     6→     6→
     7→     7→# 1. Project Vision
     8→     8→
     9→     9→Design and develop a premium, modern, international web platform called **SkilledSphere**.
    10→    10→
    11→    11→SkilledSphere is a global professional discovery and editorial platform designed to introduce exceptional professionals, industry experts, researchers, entrepreneurs, executives, founders, and leaders across different industries.
    12→    12→
    13→    13→The platform combines three major concepts:
    14→    14→
    15→    15→1. **Professional Discovery:** Helping companies, organizations, investors, and individuals discover professionals based on expertise, industry, achievements, and geographic location.
    16→    16→2. **Editorial Media:** Publishing professionally curated profiles, interviews, success stories, leadership features, and industry-related content.
    17→    17→3. **Professional Networking:** Creating opportunities for professionals and organizations to discover each other and establish meaningful business connections.
    18→    18→
    19→    19→### Core Product Positioning
    20→    20→
    21→    21→SkilledSphere is NOT a LinkedIn clone.
    22→    22→
    23→    23→It is not intended to be a social network based on daily posts, likes, comments, follower counts, or connection metrics.
    24→    24→
    25→    25→Instead, position it as a curated global professional discovery platform with a premium editorial identity.
    26→    26→
    27→    27→**Core Brand Message:**
    28→    28→
    29→    29→"Discover the People Shaping What's Next."
    30→    30→
    31→    31→The primary audience includes technology professionals, business leaders, researchers, entrepreneurs, engineers, creative professionals, medical specialists, and other recognized experts.
    32→    32→
    33→    33→The platform must be international from the beginning, with English as its primary language and an architecture that supports additional languages in the future.
    34→    34→

    51→    51→

    53→    53→
    54→    54→Use the following palette:
    55→    55→
    56→    56→- Midnight Navy: #0B1530
    57→    57→- Royal Navy: #172747
    58→    58→- Deep Blue: #263B60
    59→    59→- Champagne Gold: #C6A96B
    60→    60→- Warm White: #F5F3EE
    61→    61→- Pure Black: #090B10
    62→    62→
    63→    63→Color guidelines:
    64→    64→
    65→    65→- Navy dominates the brand identity and major interface elements.
    66→    66→- Black is used for selected immersive and cinematic sections.
    67→    67→- White and warm off-white provide contrast and readability.
    68→    68→- Gold is used sparingly for premium highlights, borders, selected labels, and important interactive details.
    69→    69→- Develop lighter and darker shades of the selected colors for visual depth.
    70→    70→
    71→    71→Avoid excessive gold surfaces.
    72→    72→

    85→    85→## UI Principles

    97→    97→# 3. Technical Architecture & Technology Stack
    98→    98→
    99→    99→Use a modern TypeScript-based architecture.
   100→   100→
   101→   101→The recommended stack is:
   102→   102→
   103→   103→| Layer | Technology |
   104→   104→|---|---|
   105→   105→| Main Framework | Next.js |
   106→   106→| UI Library | React |
   107→   107→| Programming Language | TypeScript |
   108→   108→| Styling | Tailwind CSS |
   109→   109→| UI Components | shadcn/ui |
   110→   110→| 3D Engine | Three.js |
   111→   111→| React 3D Integration | React Three Fiber |
   112→   112→| 3D Utilities | @react-three/drei |
   113→   113→| UI Animations | Framer Motion |
   114→   114→| Scroll Animations | GSAP + ScrollTrigger |
   115→   115→| CMS & Admin | Payload CMS |
   116→   116→| Database | PostgreSQL |
   117→   117→| Geospatial Extension | PostGIS, when required |
   118→   118→| Initial Search | PostgreSQL Full-Text Search |
   119→   119→| Advanced Search | Meilisearch, when required |
   120→   120→| Media Storage | Cloudflare R2 |
   121→   121→| CDN | Cloudflare CDN |
   122→   122→| Deployment | Vercel or suitable managed infrastructure |
   123→   123→
   124→   124→## Architectural Approach
   125→   125→
   126→   126→Use a **Modular Monolith** architecture.
   127→   127→
   128→   128→Do not introduce microservices, Kubernetes, or unnecessary infrastructure complexity in the initial implementation.
   129→   129→
   130→   130→The system should be modular, maintainable, and capable of evolving as the platform grows.
   131→   131→
   132→   132→Keep business logic, data access, API functionality, and presentation components logically separated.
   133→   133→
   134→   134→### Next.js
   135→   135→
   136→   136→Use Next.js App Router.
   137→   137→
   138→   138→Requirements:
   139→   139→
   140→   140→- Server-side rendering where appropriate.
   141→   141→- Static generation for suitable editorial content.
   142→   142→- Dynamic rendering for frequently updated content.
   143→   143→- SEO-friendly public pages.
   144→   144→- Dynamic professional profile routes.
   145→   145→- Optimized image delivery.
   146→   146→- API integration.
   147→   147→- Proper loading and error states.
   148→   148→
   149→   149→Use server components by default and client components only where interactivity is required.
   150→   150→
   151→   151→### Payload CMS
   152→   152→
   153→   153→Use Payload CMS as the primary content management system.
   154→   154→
   155→   155→Integrate Payload with the Next.js application.
   156→   156→
   157→   157→Payload must manage:
   158→   158→
   159→   159→- Professional profiles.
   160→   160→- Expertise categories.
   161→   161→- Industries.
   162→   162→- Countries.
   163→   163→- Professional collections.
   164→   164→- Editorial stories.
   165→   165→- Featured-professional schedules.
   166→   166→- User-submitted applications.
   167→   167→- Media assets.
   168→   168→- Administrative roles and permissions.
   169→   169→
   170→   170→Use Payload's PostgreSQL adapter.
   171→   171→
   172→   172→Build reusable collections, relationships, validation rules, access controls, and administrative workflows.
   173→   173→
   174→   174→The administration interface should be suitable for non-technical editorial staff.
   175→   175→
   176→   176→Do not build a separate administration frontend unless there is a clear requirement.
   177→   177→
   178→   178→### PostgreSQL
   179→   179→
   180→   180→Use PostgreSQL as the primary database.
   181→   181→
   182→   182→Design normalized and maintainable data relationships.
   183→   183→
   184→   184→Recommended main entities:
   185→   185→
   186→   186→- Users
   187→   187→- Professionals
   188→   188→- Countries
   189→   189→- Cities
   190→   190→- ExpertiseCategories
   191→   191→- Industries
   192→   192→- Collections
   193→   193→- CollectionMembers
   194→   194→- Stories
   195→   195→- FeaturedSchedules
   196→   196→- FeaturedApplications
   197→   197→- MediaAssets
   198→   198→
   199→   199→Support many-to-many relationships where professionals belong to multiple expertise categories, industries, or collections.
   200→   200→
   201→   201→Use stable identifiers and appropriate indexing.
   202→   202→
   203→   203→### Geospatial Data
   204→   204→
   205→   205→Support geographic information for professionals.
   206→   206→
   207→   207→Recommended fields:
   208→   208→
   209→   209→- Country code.
   210→   210→- Country name.
   211→   211→- City.
   212→   212→- Region.
   213→   213→- Latitude.
   214→   214→- Longitude.
   215→   215→
   216→   216→PostGIS can be introduced for advanced geographic queries, regional searches, and proximity-based discovery.
   217→   217→
   218→   218→Do not make PostGIS a mandatory dependency for simple country-level filtering.
   219→   219→
   220→   220→The globe must consume structured geographic data rather than hardcoded frontend information.
   221→   221→
   222→   222→### Search
   223→   223→
   224→   224→Initially use PostgreSQL Full-Text Search and indexed database queries.
   225→   225→
   226→   226→Search should cover:
   227→   227→
   228→   228→- Professional names.
   229→   229→- Professional titles.
   230→   230→- Expertise.
   231→   231→- Industries.
   232→   232→- Countries.
   233→   233→- Collections.
   234→   234→- Editorial stories.
   235→   235→
   236→   236→Keep the search architecture extensible so Meilisearch can be integrated later without redesigning the entire search experience.
   237→   237→
   238→   238→### Media Storage
   239→   239→
   240→   240→Use Cloudflare R2 for professional portraits, article images, and other uploaded media.
   241→   241→
   242→   242→Implement optimized image delivery, suitable formats, responsive image sizes, and CDN caching.
   243→   243→
   244→   244→Never store large media files directly inside the relational database.
   245→   245→
   246→   246→### ORM & Data Access
   247→   247→
   248→   248→Use Payload's database integration as the primary data-access layer.
   249→   249→
   250→   250→Avoid adding Prisma or another ORM unnecessarily.
   251→   251→
   252→   252→If independent application modules eventually require direct database access, introduce an additional data-access layer only when justified.

   297→   297→

   299→   299→

   301→   301→

   305→   305→

   307→   307→
   308→   308→Include:
   309→   309→
   310→   310→- Professional portrait.
   311→   311→- Full name.
   312→   312→- Professional title.
   313→   313→- Short biography or career summary.
   314→   314→- Main expertise.
   315→   315→- Industry.
   316→   316→- Country or location.
   317→   317→- Selected achievements.
   318→   318→- View Profile button.
   319→   319→- Featured Professional or Person of the Day label.
   320→   320→
   321→   321→The featured professional must be dynamically selected by an administrator.
   322→   322→
   323→   323→Administrators should be able to:
   324→   324→
   325→   325→- Select a professional for a specific date.
   326→   326→- Schedule future featured professionals.
   327→   327→- Change or remove the currently featured person.
   328→   328→- Preview the featured card before publishing.
   329→   329→
   330→   330→Support a daily rotation system.
   331→   331→
   332→   332→Do not hardcode the featured professional in frontend components.
   333→   333→
   334→   334→Use realistic sample data for the initial prototype.
   335→   335→
   336→   336→## Section 2: Interactive 3D Global Globe
   337→   337→
   338→   338→Create a large, realistic, interactive 3D globe as the central visual identity of the homepage.
   339→   339→
   340→   340→Initially, position the globe behind and slightly to the left of the featured-professional card.
   341→   341→
   342→   342→### Globe Visual Design
   343→   343→
   344→   344→Match the SkilledSphere visual identity.
   345→   345→
   346→   346→Use:
   347→   347→
   348→   348→- Dark navy and deep blue oceans.
   349→   349→- Subtle country boundaries.
   350→   350→- Elegant geographic outlines.
   351→   351→- Carefully selected gold highlights.
   352→   352→- Subtle illuminated geographic markers.
   353→   353→- Soft atmospheric lighting.
   354→   354→- Sophisticated dark surroundings.
   355→   355→
   356→   356→Avoid cartoon-like earth graphics and excessive neon effects.
   357→   357→
   358→   358→The globe should rotate slowly and continuously when idle.
   359→   359→
   360→   360→Its movement should feel calm, elegant, and cinematic.
   361→   361→
   362→   362→### Scroll-Driven Globe Transition
   363→   363→
   364→   364→This is a critical interaction.
   365→   365→
   366→   366→Implement a coordinated scroll-based transition between the featured-professional hero and the global discovery experience.
   367→   367→
   368→   368→Initial state:
   369→   369→
   370→   370→- Featured card is prominent.
   371→   371→- Globe is positioned behind the card.
   372→   372→- Globe rotates slowly.
   373→   373→
   374→   374→As the user scrolls:
   375→   375→
   376→   376→1. The featured card gradually scales down.
   377→   377→2. The card moves backward visually and fades away.
   378→   378→3. The globe gradually increases in size.
   379→   379→4. The globe moves into the foreground.
   380→   380→5. The globe becomes the dominant visual element.
   381→   381→6. The transition feels continuous and cinematic.
   382→   382→
   383→   383→Avoid excessive animation or interactions that make scrolling uncomfortable.
   384→   384→
   385→   385→Once the transition completes, the globe becomes fully interactive.
   386→   386→
   387→   387→### Interactive Country Selection
   388→   388→
   389→   389→Users must be able to interact directly with the globe.
   390→   390→
   391→   391→Required interactions:
   392→   392→
   393→   393→- Rotate the globe using mouse drag or touch gestures.
   394→   394→- Zoom in and out where appropriate.
   395→   395→- Click or tap a country.
   396→   396→- Highlight the selected country.
   397→   397→- Rotate the globe toward a selected country.
   398→   398→- Display a country-specific professional discovery panel.
   399→   399→
   400→   400→When a country is selected, open a floating panel on the RIGHT side of the globe.
   401→   401→
   402→   402→The panel should contain:
   403→   403→
   404→   404→- Country name and flag.
   405→   405→- Number of listed professionals.
   406→   406→- Featured professionals from that country.
   407→   407→- Professional portraits.
   408→   408→- Names and professional titles.
   409→   409→- Main expertise tags.
   410→   410→- Short descriptions.
   411→   411→- Links to individual profiles.
   412→   412→- View All Professionals action.
   413→   413→
   414→   414→The panel must feel visually integrated with the globe environment.
   415→   415→
   416→   416→Do not make it look like a generic dashboard.
   417→   417→
   418→   418→### Geographic Data Management
   419→   419→
   420→   420→The globe must connect to structured professional data.
   421→   421→
   422→   422→Each professional record must contain geographic information.
   423→   423→
   424→   424→When an administrator adds a new professional and assigns a country, that professional should automatically appear in the corresponding geographic discovery results.
   425→   425→
   426→   426→Do not require manual editing of the 3D globe whenever a professional is added.
   427→   427→
   428→   428→Countries without listed professionals should remain selectable and display an elegant empty state.
   429→   429→
   430→   430→Use real geographic boundaries and country identifiers.
   431→   431→
   432→   432→### Country Navigation Ticker
   433→   433→
   434→   434→Directly below the globe section, create an elegant horizontal country navigation ticker.
   435→   435→
   436→   436→Example:
   437→   437→
   438→   438→United States · United Kingdom · Germany · France · Canada · Japan · Singapore · Australia · Switzerland
   439→   439→
   440→   440→When users click a country:
   441→   441→
   442→   442→- The globe smoothly rotates toward that country.
   443→   443→- The selected country becomes highlighted.
   444→   444→- The professional panel opens.
   445→   445→- The country ticker reflects the active selection.
   446→   446→
   447→   447→Support manual horizontal scrolling.
   448→   448→
   449→   449→The ticker should feel like part of the visual experience rather than a conventional navigation menu.
   450→   450→
   451→   451→### Leaving the Globe Section
   452→   452→
   453→   453→As the user continues scrolling:
   454→   454→
   455→   455→- The globe gradually scales down.
   456→   456→- It moves backward and fades away.
   457→   457→- The country ticker disappears.
   458→   458→- The next homepage section enters naturally.
   459→   459→
   460→   460→Use coordinated scroll animations.
   461→   461→
   462→   462→The globe must not remain fixed indefinitely or interfere with subsequent sections.
